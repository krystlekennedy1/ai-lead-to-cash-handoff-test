Across the 284 deals that reached kickoff, the median closed-won to kickoff time is 9 days (mean 9.8), and the time is lost in two places: the wait for credit check and the wait for kickoff after invoicing.

Stage | Avg days | Share of all elapsed days
Closed-won to order review | 0.7 | 7%
Order review to credit check (includes legal) | 3.2 | 32%
Credit check to booked | 0.6 | 6%
Booked to invoice | 1.5 | 16%
Invoice to kickoff | 3.9 | 39%

1. Order review to credit check is where the long waits come from. The average is 3.2 days, but 21% of deals wait 5 or more days, and those deals account for 55% of all time spent in this stage. Two things drive it:
Nonstandard terms (20% of deals). Legal alone averages 3.7 days after order review, then 3.3 more days before credit check starts. These deals take 13.3 days end to end, versus 8.9 for standard ones.
Deal size. Deals of $100K or more take 4.5 days to reach credit check even with standard terms, versus 1.6 days for smaller deals. This looks like a queue or approval threshold, not complexity. Large deals with nonstandard terms are the worst case: 37 deals averaging 14.7 days, and 68% of them take 14 or more days.

2. Invoice to kickoff averages 3.9 days regardless of segment, size or terms. Most deals land at 2 to 5 days, so it looks like a scheduling cadence or CS capacity limit. It's 39% of all elapsed time, and a third of deals wait 5 or more days.

3. Rework doesn't slow cycle time. Deals with rework average 9.8 days, the same as those without. It is concentrated in Enterprise (0.55 rework events per deal versus 0.18 in SMB), and 4 of the 8 deals with 3 or more events are Enterprise. It costs effort and quality, not calendar time.

4. Segment gap. SMB takes 8.6 days, Commercial 10.4 and Enterprise 13.2. Almost all of that gap is the credit and legal stage, because 50% of Enterprise deals have nonstandard terms versus 8% in SMB.

What I'd fix, in order of impact
Start credit check at closed-won, in parallel with legal. Credit currently waits for legal to finish on nonstandard deals, and that handoff alone averages 3.3 days. Running them together takes a few days off roughly 1 in 5 deals.
Set an SLA and a fast lane for large deals' credit check. If the $100K+ queue ran as fast as the small-deal queue, it would remove about 3 days from 86 deals.
Pre-approved clause library and a legal triage rule. Route common nonstandard terms through standard fallbacks, and escalate to full legal review only when a term is truly new.
Book kickoff at the invoice step, or earlier, with a 2-day target. Today it's scheduled after invoicing, and that is the largest single block of time.
Treat Enterprise rework as a quality problem. A deal-desk checklist at order review should cut repeat handoffs, even though it won't move cycle time much.
